Lehulum ለሁሉም

One lesson. Every learner.

An accessible-media plug-in for ALX Savanna and any learning platform: captions in Amharic and English, an Ethiopian Sign Language window, audio description, Braille and lessons that work on 3G.

  • 37 KB script
  • 0 trackers or CDNs
  • No login
  • Works offline

Ermias · Estifanos · Meareg

The problem

An ordinary online lesson shuts three groups out

Deaf and hard of hearing

  • Audio-only lectures: no captions, no signer
  • English-only speech leaves Amharic-first learners behind
  • Captions that cover code and slides

Blind and low vision

  • Diagrams and code on screen have no description
  • Unlabelled buttons and keyboard traps break screen readers
  • No Braille or structured handout

Slow, costly networks

  • Our 90-second test video is 34 MB
  • Mobile data is expensive, so learners drop the course
  • Power cuts and dropped signals stop streaming

Mission: any learner, whatever their disability, language or network, can finish an ALX Savanna lesson with dignity.

Who we built for

Three learners, one lesson

Hana

Adama · Ethiopian Sign Language

Audio-only lectures give her no way in.

A sign-language window kept in step with the video, plus Amharic captions.

Dawit

Bahir Dar · TalkBack and NVDA

His screen reader cannot see diagrams or code.

The video pauses and the description is read out. A full transcript, 44 px targets.

Selam

Hawassa · 3G Android phone

Streaming video uses up her airtime in minutes.

Audio-only data saver, and the whole lesson as one 8 KB file.

Built for disability access, useful to everyone on a weak connection or a cheap phone. These are personas, not people we met.

The solution

Two lines of code in any lesson page

<lehulum-player lesson="lesson-001"
  api="..." lang="en"></lehulum-player>

<script src="lehulum.js" defer></script>
  • A web component: no framework, and the host's styles cannot break the controls.
  • Our own Ethiopic font: no Google Fonts, no CDN.
  • A synchronised sign window: movable, resizable, keyboard friendly.
  • 37 KBgzipped script
  • 0outside requests
  • 44 pxminimum touch target
  • 64 kbpsaudio-only stream

The same two lines work in ALX Savanna, Moodle, Canvas or a plain HTML page.

Feature 1

Captions in Amharic and English

Switch language instantly

  • Amharic and English change on the fly, with no video reload.
  • Self-hosted Noto Sans Ethiopic, so every glyph shows on any device.
  • A caption band under the controls: it never covers code or slide headings.
  • At most two short lines at a time.

The learner chooses how it looks

  • Text size: normal, large, extra large.
  • Colours: default, yellow on black, white on black.
  • Keyboard shortcuts can be turned off, so nothing fires by accident.

Video, controls and captions fit on one screen, with no scrolling during the lesson.

Feature 2

A sign-language window that follows the lesson

Kept in step

  • Play, pause, seek and speed changes carry over to the sign video.
  • If it drifts more than 0.3 seconds, it is pulled back.
  • Set up for Ethiopian Sign Language (ase-ET) with a credit line for the interpreter.
  • With the data saver on, it loads only when asked.

Under the learner's control

  • Dock in any corner or float it.
  • Move, Bigger and Smaller are plain buttons, so no dragging is needed (WCAG 2.5.7).
  • Three sizes, so hands and faces stay easy to read.

Honest status: the clip in our demo is a placeholder. A real interpreter, working with the Ethiopian National Association of the Deaf, is the first thing we need.

Feature 3

Made for 3G and power cuts

Data saver

2.4 MB → 0.8 MB

  • Audio-only 64 kbps stream; captions and transcript stay in sync
  • Load the video with one click when you want it

Read the lesson

No video at all

  • A full transcript with timestamps; a click jumps the video there
  • Works with a screen reader and no sound

Save for offline

About 8 KB

  • One self-contained lesson file, downloaded in resumable pieces
  • Opens with no internet, no login, no tracking

Download on campus Wi-Fi, study at home when the power is out.

Feature 4

The instructor adds one video; the rest is drafted

  1. UploadA lecture video from the instructor's computer
  2. Audioffmpeg makes the low-data audio-only copy
  3. SpeechVoice detection finds where speech starts and stops
  4. DraftWhisper writes timed captions; Amharic is drafted
  5. SignAttach a sign video, preview it in sync

Then a person approves every line, and only approved text reaches a learner. In our 90-second test video the machine made 13 caption drafts and one mistake, hearing “A I” as “A E I”.

The Instructor Studio on the demo site shows these steps and the review table. It does not process new videos there.

Learning to livelihood

A certificate anyone can verify, and jobs in plain language

Check and certificate

  • A short check question at the end of the lesson
  • A certificate signed with Ed25519, in the Open Badges 3.0 format
  • Anyone can verify it from a link. It holds no personal data.

Accessible job cards

  • A job posting turned into a three-line summary
  • Deadlines also in the Ethiopian calendar
  • Accommodations shown up front: relay calls, sign interpreters, screen-reader setup
  • 10 cards, 5 English and 5 Amharic, with spoken summaries

Learn, prove it, apply: the lesson leads to a credential and to work.

Adoption and privacy

Plugs into any platform, keeps learners' data safe

Front end

  • One web component, no framework
  • Isolated from the host's CSS
  • Progress and complete events, so Moodle, Canvas or Savanna can track completion

Back end

  • FastAPI, with an OpenAPI file
  • Video seeking works (range requests)
  • Or no server at all: plain files on any web host, like this demo

Privacy

  • No trackers, no analytics
  • No font or script CDNs
  • A certificate check reveals no personal data

One command, docker compose up, runs the whole system.
